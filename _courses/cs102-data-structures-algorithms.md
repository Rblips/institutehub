credits: 4

semester: "Spring"
level: "Undergraduate (Core)"

prerequisites: "CS-101: Introduction to Computer Programming"
lecture_hours: 3

description: "Foundational study of linear and non-linear data structures, asymptotic time/space complexity analysis, balanced search trees, graph algorithms, dynamic programming, and amortized bounds."
topics:
  - module: "Module 1"
    title: "Asymptotic Complexity & Amortized Analysis"
    description: "Big-O, Omega, Theta notation, recurrence relations, Master theorem, potential method."
  - module: "Module 2"
    title: "Trees & Balanced Hierarchies"
    description: "AVL trees, Red-Black trees, B-Trees, heaps, and priority queues."
  - module: "Module 3"
    title: "Graph Traversal & Shortest Paths"
    description: "BFS, DFS, Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford, Floyd-Warshall, minimum spanning trees."
  - module: "Module 4"
    title: "Algorithmic Paradigms"
    description: "Divide-and-conquer, greedy algorithms, dynamic programming, and NP-completeness intro."
textbooks:
  - "Cormen, T. H., Leiserson, C. E., Rivest, R. L., & Stein, C. (2022). Introduction to Algorithms (4th ed.). MIT Press."
  - "Kleinberg, J., & Tardos, É. (2006). Algorithm Design. Pearson."
---

This course serves as the foundational pillar for computer science undergraduates, equipping students with the theoretical and practical skills required to design, analyze, and implement highly efficient algorithmic solutions to computational problems.
